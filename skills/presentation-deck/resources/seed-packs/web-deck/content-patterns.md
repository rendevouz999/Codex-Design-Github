# Web Deck Content Patterns

## Copy Rule

- Every headline is a takeaway, not a label. `Revenue` is a label; `Revenue doubled on the same headcount` is a takeaway.
- One claim per slide. A second claim is a second slide, not a second column.
- Write for the medium: a speaker-led deck is read in three seconds per glance, a reading-first deck is read without a voice.
- Support lines carry evidence, not restatement. If a bullet repeats the headline, delete it.
- Reveal order is the argument order. Write the steps in the sequence they are meant to land.

## Per Archetype

- `cover`: eyebrow names the category or audience, headline states the claim, subtitle names the context, meta row carries presenter and date.
- `agenda`: parallel verb-led or noun-led phrasing across all rows, three to six rows, no row longer than six words, no row that is only a section number.
- `section-divider`: section title plus one caption line naming what the next run proves. No bullets.
- `problem`: headline names the cost of the current state; each friction block names who pays it and how often.
- `solution`: headline answers the problem headline in the same grammar; three steps named as actions, not features.
- `feature`: card title is a benefit, card body is the mechanism in one sentence. Never title a card with a product noun alone.
- `data`: headline is the chart conclusion; readout values use number, unit, and comparison base; source line names dataset and period.
- `dashboard`: KPI label states the measure, value states the level, delta states direction and window. Panels get one title each, no sub-headline.
- `timeline`: node date, node label, one detail line. Past tense throughout; a future node belongs on `roadmap`.
- `roadmap`: phase label plus window, lane items as committed scope in future tense. No phase without a window.
- `case-study`: client and sector as eyebrow, headline as the result, three blocks as context, action, result, and one metric band as proof.
- `testimonial`: one quote, trimmed to the sentence that carries the point, ellipsis only where a clause was cut. Attribution is name, role, company. Never paraphrase a quote.
- `team`: name, role, and one credential line that is relevant to this deck. No biography paragraphs.
- `pricing`: tier name states the buyer, price states the unit and period, feature rows are parallel in length and order across tiers, footnote carries the billing condition.
- `portfolio`: project title, sector, and year. Let the work speak; no benefit copy over the frames.
- `closing`: headline is the one thing to remember, next-step line is the one thing to do, contact row is the path to do it.

## Metric Framing

- Four parts: number, unit, comparison base, implication. A number with no base is decoration.
- Name the window. `Up 40 percent` is incomplete; `up 40 percent year over year` is a claim.
- Round to the precision the audience can hold. Three significant figures is usually one too many on a stage.
- Put the implication in the headline, not in a footnote.

## Quote Framing

- Quote first, attribution second, portrait last. Never lead with the logo.
- One quote per slide, and no more than three testimonial slides in a deck.
- Keep the speaker voice; fix only punctuation and obvious transcription noise.

## Agenda Phrasing

- Use one grammatical shape for the whole list.
- Match agenda row labels to the section dividers that follow them, word for word.
- Do not number the agenda rows differently from the divider numerals.

## Notes Field

- Speaker notes carry the spoken version, not a copy of the slide text.
- Write notes as delivery cues: the transition in, the point to land, the question to expect.
- Keep notes out of the stage. They belong in `notes` and, when enabled, the notes pane.
