# Figma Professional Template Learning

This is the professional designer reading protocol for learning from Figma design-template references.

The goal is to build Codex's design cognition from reference systems, not from shallow appearance matching. A Figma reference is a blueprint: read the product purpose, structure, visual logic, source-file system, and buyer value, then reconstruct a new original template.

## Core Mindset

- Do not study a Figma template only as a pretty screen.
- Do not start from color, hero layout, or a generic section stack.
- Do not use "minimalist" as the safety word when the reference is unclear.
- Read the template as a product system: what it sells, who buys it, why the structure works, and how a buyer can edit it.
- Reuse professional thinking patterns, not recognizable execution.

## Mandatory Reading Order

For every Figma template reference, complete this reading order before structure planning:

1. Product purpose and niche
   - Identify whether the reference is SaaS, ecommerce, luxury interior, creative agency, finance, wedding, restaurant, hotel, real estate, dashboard, booking, corporate, editorial, or another product family.
   - Explain what the template helps the buyer sell, prove, or operate.

2. Visual direction
   - Classify the main direction: clean, luxury, bold creative, editorial, corporate, futuristic tech, playful, vintage, dark premium, pastel, or another specific mood.
   - Treat this as art direction, not a shortcut to fewer sections.

3. Section structure
   - Map hero, nav, proof, about, features, services, product/showcase, benefits, process, testimonials, pricing/packages, FAQ, closing CTA, and footer only when they fit the category.
   - Do not force one universal order across references.

4. Grid and layout system
   - Read frame width, margins, column behavior, section spacing, card spacing, alignment, overlap, broken-grid use, and intentional off-grid moves.
   - Premium quality often comes from proportion and spacing before color.

5. Typography hierarchy
   - Read heading/body/caption roles, scale, weight, line height, casing, letter spacing, and whether the type feels editorial, tech, luxury, corporate, playful, or utilitarian.

6. Color-role system
   - Read background, alternate section background, main text, secondary text, CTA, badge, border, card, accent, and footer roles.
   - Learn the function of the palette. Do not copy the palette.

7. Image direction
   - Read image type, crop, angle, lighting, full-bleed behavior, masking, collage, product shots, realism, illustration, 3D, or editorial use.
   - Convert source-template image needs into editable placeholders unless generated imagery was explicitly requested.

8. Component style
   - Read buttons, cards, badges, icons, forms, navigation, testimonial cards, pricing cards, image frames, footer links, dividers, and state behavior.
   - Components are part of product quality, not optional decoration.

9. Spacing rhythm
   - Read section gaps, card padding, heading-to-paragraph spacing, paragraph-to-button spacing, list spacing, and text/image distance.
   - Spacing must feel rhythmic, not random.

10. Visual uniqueness
   - Identify the signature: oversized type, diagonal image, floating card, sidebar nav, circular crop, editorial grid, split screen, collage, marquee, abstract shape, big numbers, or horizontal showcase.
   - Rebuild the principle with a new composition, content rhythm, palette, and motif.

11. Content logic
   - Read what the headline sells, what the subheadline clarifies, where CTAs go, what objections are answered, how proof appears, and whether pricing/footer logic fits the niche.
   - Dummy content weakens template value.

12. Responsive behavior
   - Infer tablet/mobile transformations: column collapse, safe image cropping, type scaling, nav behavior, spacing compression, and interaction simplification.
   - Marketplace templates feel stronger when responsive behavior is planned from the start.

13. Figma source hygiene
   - Check layer naming, section frames, auto-layout use, reusable components, variants, color styles, text styles, spacing consistency, and whether groups/layers are organized.
   - A sellable Figma template must be comfortable for the buyer to edit.

14. Default-template syndrome
   - Reject repeated hero-left/image-right, repeated 3-card sections, identical CTA/testimonial/pricing layouts, SaaS-card behavior in non-SaaS categories, and clean-empty pages without character.

15. Original reconstruction
   - State what will be learned, what will not be copied, and how the new template changes structure, composition, color roles, component grouping, content logic, image direction, and responsive/Figma behavior.

## Product-Family Reading Cues

- SaaS or product website: dashboard/product proof, feature depth, testimonials, pricing, integrations, security, FAQ, and trial/demo CTA.
- Furniture, interior, real estate, hotel, resort, or luxury lifestyle: large imagery, refined typography, material/location proof, whitespace as prestige, detail sections, gallery, packages/availability, and booking/contact logic.
- Creative agency or studio: portfolio evidence, flexible grid, bold or editorial composition, case-study rhythm, process, services, client proof, and expressive but controlled typography.
- Ecommerce or retail: product discovery, category logic, product cards, offer/signals, social proof, editorial product storytelling, cart/account trust, and footer support.
- Restaurant, wedding, event, or hospitality: emotional first impression, photo direction, venue/menu/package/story sections, booking path, testimonial proof, FAQ, and contact/location closure.
- Finance, fintech, business, or corporate: trust, clarity, risk reduction, metrics, product/service explanation, compliance/support cues, expert proof, and precise CTA hierarchy.
- Dashboard, POS, admin, or operational product: navigation shell, data density, tables, filters, charts, states, empty/loading/error behavior, settings, and reusable control systems.
- Blockchain, AI, futuristic tech, or developer product: abstract/technical visual language, ecosystem diagram, product mechanism, security/trust, roadmap, use cases, integration, and proof without empty hype.

## Evidence Requirement

Every professional-reading claim must include evidence or low-confidence status:

- Observed evidence: what was visible in the Figma metadata, screenshot, frame tree, layer names, page names, or reference image.
- Inferred designer reason: why a professional designer likely made that choice.
- Buyer effect: what the choice makes the buyer or end user feel or understand.
- Reusable logic: what can transfer into a new design.
- Original transformation: what must change so the output is not a copy.
- Confidence: high, medium, or low.

## Premium Without Minimalist Dependence

Premium does not mean default minimalist.

Premium may be:

- quiet luxury with strong imagery and spacing discipline
- dense finance with trust hierarchy and controlled information
- bold agency with expressive layout and portfolio proof
- editorial ecommerce with image rhythm and strong product discovery
- operational dashboard with compact clarity and state coverage

Use "minimalist" only when the reference evidence truly shows intentional minimalism. If the product family needs depth, proof, or components, build them.

## Section Density Decision Rule

Do not decide section density from mood words alone.

Choose section behavior from buyer friction:

- Calm sections orient the viewer, create breathing room, or let premium imagery and positioning land.
- Dense sections carry feature proof, comparison, scope, operational detail, product taxonomy, or buying evidence.
- Visual-led sections create desire, brand memory, lifestyle imagination, product context, or emotional authority.
- Proof-heavy sections reduce risk through testimonials, metrics, certifications, security, returns, process clarity, or social proof.

Each section should have a job: desire, orientation, proof, comparison, objection handling, transaction, or closure. The product family decides the rhythm.

## Figma Component Balance Rule

Componentize repeated, buyer-edited, stateful, or system-defining elements: nav, footer, buttons, CTA bands, cards, pricing/product cards, testimonial blocks, forms, badges, reusable media frames, and repeated list rows.

Do not componentize every unique editorial section just to look systematic. Unique sections may stay as clearly named auto-layout frames when componentization would make the file harder for a buyer to edit.

## Multi-Page Completeness Rule

When a reference is a full website kit or multi-page Figma system, separate the jobs:

- Homepage job: positioning, first impression, proof, offer/service/product path, objection handling, and conversion closure.
- Inner-page job: service detail, portfolio or case study depth, product/category detail, contact, about/process, blog/content, or other niche-specific decision support.
- Source-product job: style guide, reusable components, clear naming, editable placeholders, documentation notes, and license/resource clarity when relevant.

Do not judge a full website reference by homepage structure alone. A homepage-only build can still be valid, but it must declare the missing inner-page assumptions instead of pretending the whole product has been covered.

## Operational State Rule

Dashboard, POS, admin, booking, fintech, and other operational templates must show real workflow behavior, not only decorative panels.

Plan for:

- navigation and current-location state
- search, filter, tab, sort, or category state
- selection, active, disabled, focus, and hover/pressed states when meaningful
- empty, loading, error, and success states where the product flow depends on feedback
- transactional summaries such as cart, billing, totals, booking details, payment, export, or confirmation

Component/state coverage should be enough to make the template feel usable and editable, but not so many variants that the buyer cannot understand the file.

## Output Requirement

Before building a new template from Figma reference material, produce or mentally complete:

- product purpose
- visual direction
- section map
- grid/layout reading
- typography hierarchy
- color-role system
- image direction
- component style
- spacing rhythm
- visual signature
- content logic
- responsive plan
- Figma source hygiene plan
- default-template syndrome risks
- original reconstruction plan

If these are missing, the design has not been professionally studied yet.

## Continuous Learning After Build

After a substantial Figma template build, the learning loop continues:

1. Compare the built template against the reference insight, not only against the visual result.
2. Record the strongest lesson and the weakest uncertainty.
3. Turn the uncertainty into at least one new question.
4. Search web evidence when the question affects template quality, marketplace readiness, Figma editability, responsive behavior, accessibility, ecommerce behavior, or design-system behavior.
5. Distill the answer into a next-build rule.
6. Update Brain Design workflow files when the rule is actionable and protected-change permission is satisfied.

The goal is not fast output. The goal is to form professional template-designer reasoning across many builds: product purpose, buyer decision, reference logic, section depth, visual rhythm, Figma source hygiene, editability, and marketplace value.
