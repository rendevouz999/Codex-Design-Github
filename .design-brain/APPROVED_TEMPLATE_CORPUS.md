## Approved Template Product Shape

The approved folders show that a sellable Figma website/template product is usually a package, not only one page.

Expected package surfaces:

- Main template page or primary website design.
- Design-system board or foundations page.
- Main cover for marketplace presentation.
- Colors and typography preview.
- Features overview or section/value overview.
- Overview-template image that proves breadth and editability.
- Figma source file.
- Help or PDF material when the product is being prepared for delivery.

Missing one of these surfaces is not automatically wrong, but Brain Design should treat the package above as the local approved standard for premium template products unless the current brief intentionally scopes smaller.

## Approved Main-Page Structure Logic

The approved website/landing templates are rarely thin. Many main pages are long, product-specific, and built from 8 or more meaningful blocks.

Recurring structural traits:

- Hero establishes a specific product promise, not a generic startup headline.
- Early proof appears quickly through metrics, status rails, booking cards, product cards, screenshots, trust strips, or category chips.
- Sections alternate calm orientation with denser proof or decision modules.
- Each section has a job: desire, orientation, proof, product discovery, service selection, process clarity, pricing/package decision, objection handling, or conversion closure.
- Category-specific modules replace universal card grids: booking forms, route planners, product catalogs, advisory plans, audit consoles, portfolio cases, menu/ticket modules, field dispatch blocks, reading/member systems, care-path cards, or operational dashboards.
- Dark or colored contrast bands are used as rhythm breaks, proof zones, quote/statement bands, CTA anchors, or footer bridges.
- Pricing, packages, forms, FAQ, objection handling, and footer closure appear when buyer friction calls for them.
- Copy is concrete and commercially useful. Avoid lorem ipsum and vague claims.

Brain Design must not reduce a rich approved product family into `hero + features + pricing + footer`. If the category has buyer friction, operational logic, product discovery, service booking, proof, or trust concerns, the structure must show that depth.

## Approved Layout Fluidity Lessons

The approved corpus shows that accepted templates are not only different by topic or palette. They differ by spatial gesture and zoomed-out silhouette.

Observed layout directions include:

- dark technical console layouts for AI/SaaS/operations
- editorial rail and magazine-well structures for publishing and commerce
- booking/form-first split systems for service and healthcare pages
- product-stage and catalog shelves for ecommerce/lifestyle templates
- hospitality story paths with large image pauses and package islands
- finance proof ledgers and advisory matrices
- route/map/task planner structures for travel and rental products
- creative/portfolio case-study runways with asymmetric media rhythm

Future templates should not repeatedly fall back to a centered vertical stack. Use `.design-brain/LAYOUT_FLUIDITY_EXPANSION.md` to define layout gesture, section silhouettes, focal path, composition tension, negative-space shape, depth layering, module scale variation, and breakout behavior before build.

## Approved Visual-Language Logic

The corpus is intentionally varied. It includes quiet luxury, editorial commerce, dark AI/SaaS, healthcare/service booking, hospitality, finance, restaurant, travel, creative portfolio, publishing, and operational rental/booking directions.

Reusable visual principles:

- Palette roles are product-specific. Do not default to blue SaaS or one neutral minimal palette.
- Accent colors have jobs: CTA, proof number, active state, pricing highlight, signal glow, product category, trust marker, or rhythm break.
- Effects are semantic, not decorative. Approved examples use glow, dark panels, soft card shadows, hairline borders, overlays, image depth, and gradient light when they support hierarchy or mood.
- Dense modules are controlled through grid, spacing, card hierarchy, and local contrast rather than random decoration.
- Editorial/lifestyle products rely on image rhythm, material color, calm whitespace, and refined type.
- Tech/AI/finance products may use dark surfaces, glowing signal accents, dashboard panels, metrics, and operational proof.
- Service/booking products prioritize calm decision paths, form clarity, trust sequencing, and visible next action.

## Approved Design-System Board Logic

The approved design systems are concise, practical, and product-specific. They usually document enough for a buyer to understand and edit the source without turning the board into a bloated enterprise design system.

Expected design-system board content:

- Template name and design-system title.
- Short positioning note or foundation summary.
- Color tokens with role names, not only decorative swatches.
- Typography scale with display, heading, body, caption, and label roles.
- Spacing, radius, shadow/effect, or layout tokens where relevant.
- Primitive examples such as buttons, chips, cards, pricing/product cards, form fields, or media frames.
- Auto-layout/source-template rules when the output is Figma.
- Accessibility guardrails or contrast/use notes.
- Image placeholder policy when image slots exist.
- Organized-layer or source-file receipt when useful.

Componentization should be deliberate. Repeated, buyer-edited, stateful, or system-defining elements should become components when the current product needs them. Unique editorial sections can remain clearly named auto-layout frames if componentization would make the file harder to edit.

## Approved Preview Packaging Logic

The preview images are part of the product value. They sell the template's quality before the buyer opens the source.

Common cover pattern:

- Large left-side product title and category.
- Browser/window crop of the actual design.
- Small tags that explain included value, such as Figma file, auto-layout, Google Fonts, or organized layers.
- Bottom metadata strip or feature rail.
- Background color tied to the template palette.

Common overview pattern:

- Show the real template clearly enough to inspect.
- Use section breakdowns, feature highlights, colors/typography panels, or source-system previews.
- Avoid abstract mockups that hide the product.
- Keep marketplace preview design as polished as the template itself.

## Approved-Corpus Workflow

Before building or critiquing a new template product:

1. Classify the product family.
2. Pick 2-4 nearest approved corpus examples when available.
3. Inspect the selected examples at three levels: main page, design system, and preview packaging.
4. Record the reusable logic:
   - buyer friction
   - section rhythm
   - proof strategy
   - visual-language role
   - design-system board content
   - preview packaging logic
5. Record the do-not-copy boundary:
   - hero grammar
   - exact section order
   - specific palette mapping
   - exact card cadence
   - copied copy rhythm
   - repeated preview composition
6. Build a new original structure fingerprint.

If no close approved match exists, use adjacent product-family examples and say the match is adjacent, not direct.
