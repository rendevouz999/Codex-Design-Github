# Website Template Checklist

## P0

- Template purpose is classified before choosing section count.
- Website product family is selected from `.design-brain/FIGMA_WEBSITE_TEMPLATE_PRODUCT_TYPES.md` before choosing section order.
- Landing page is selected only for explicit landing/campaign/lead-gen/sales-page/waitlist intent.
- Product-type standard count is checked before design. If the standard is unclear, do web/category research before building.
- Buyer, end user, and marketplace category are explicit.
- Structure count is justified by product type, not by reduced-scope taste.
- Clean visual direction does not reduce product depth.
- Old task-specific omissions such as desktop-only, no-component, no-cover/preview, or no-mobile are not reused as persistent bans; output surfaces are chosen from the current request, product standard, and buyer value.
- Full website templates include a homepage block map and product-family page/screen map.
- Landing pages are treated as a focused subset, not the default for every website template.
- Generic website, SaaS website, business website, ecommerce, portfolio, blog, docs, dashboard, listing/directory, education, event, and travel briefs must not route to landing page by default.
- Hero/header kits are clearly labeled as kits when they do not include full website structure.
- Ecommerce and dashboard templates include the expected transactional or operational screens.
- Responsive desktop, tablet, and mobile logic is planned.

## P1

- Homepage has enough trust, proof, detail, and conversion coverage for the buyer.
- Inner pages cover real buyer reuse: about, service/detail, portfolio/detail, blog/detail, contact, FAQ, pricing, auth, account, or checkout as relevant.
- Component states are present where interaction exists: forms, nav, filters, table rows, product cards, auth, errors.
- Marketplace preview can prove template breadth and editability.

## Hard Gate

- Do not approve a website template that only has hero, features, pricing, FAQ, CTA, and footer unless the product is explicitly a narrow landing page.
- Do not approve a plan that says "website template" but does not name one of the 16 website product families.
- Do not call a reduced structure "premium" when it simply omits required buyer journey steps.
- Do not approve a sellable website template unless it has standard depth for its product type, usually 10-14 product-specific blocks for serious service/SaaS/trust-heavy pages or a clear reason for a narrower landing-page scope.
- Do not let originality replace completeness. A template can be different from the reference and still fail because it is underbuilt.
