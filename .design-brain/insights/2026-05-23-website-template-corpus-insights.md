## Main Lesson

Website design intelligence must start by diagnosing the product subtype. A travel landing page, ecommerce product page, dashboard UI kit, newsletter, portfolio, mobile app screen, and hero/header kit should not share the same structure just because they are all distributed as "website templates".

The strongest reusable lesson from this corpus is this:

- Website templates sell clarity in the first viewport.
- The first viewport must reveal category, buyer use case, and quality fast.
- The rest of the page must prove the promise through the right product evidence for that subtype.
- Similar visual mood can be preserved while changing section order, composition, grouping, media strategy, and rhythm.

## Corpus Archetypes

### Landing Page / Lead Generation

Observed examples include travel, business, app development, lead generation, industrial, hosting, AI, wellness, rental, and shop-opening pages.

Reusable insight:
- A landing page is a conversion sequence, not a stack of decorative sections.
- The page needs an entry promise, proof, mechanism, offer, trust, objection handling, and final action.
- Section order should change by niche. A travel page can open with destination proof; a SaaS page can open with product evidence; a rental page can open with search/filter utility; a wellness page can open with human trust and appointment access.

Do not copy:
- Hero, feature cards, testimonial, pricing, FAQ, CTA in the same order for every niche.
- One isometric illustration as the only product signal.
- Generic CTA repetition without new information.

### Hero / Header Kits

Observed examples include business hero, Conlar, green inflation header, header kit, hijab sale, and banner templates.

Reusable insight:
- A header kit is a first-viewport product, not a complete website.
- Its value comes from precise title/media/CTA choreography, strong category signal, and multiple usable header variants.

Do not copy:
- Treating a single hero as a full landing page.
- Reusing the same left-text/right-image split as the default answer.

### Ecommerce / Product Pages

Observed examples include BIM store, jewelry, Minolta, modern product page, open new shop, shopping list, and hijab sale.

Reusable insight:
- Ecommerce templates need product inspection, browse behavior, price/value hierarchy, category navigation, variant or filter logic, and purchase confidence.
- Product imagery should carry the page; decorative illustration is secondary.

Do not copy:
- Agency-style feature cards replacing product discovery.
- Product pages without price, variants, gallery logic, or buying confidence cues.

### Dashboard / UI Kit

Observed examples include teacher dashboard, game store dashboard, file management dashboard, campaign dashboard, and blog/UI kit sections.

Reusable insight:
- Dashboard products are operational interfaces. They need app shell, navigation, KPI hierarchy, tables, filters, charts, statuses, empty/loading/error states, and density rules.
- Visual polish should support scanning and repeated work, not dramatic marketing composition.

Do not copy:
- Landing-page hero logic inside dashboards.
- Chart cards without labels, legends, comparison meaning, or state coverage.

### Agency / Portfolio / Resume

Observed examples include clean resume, creative portfolio, digital studio, Gokil, Joy, and photographer templates.

Reusable insight:
- Portfolio templates must make the work visible early.
- The best portfolio logic balances identity, selected work, case details, services, credibility, and contact.
- Editorial pacing, asymmetric project crops, and a clear case-study rhythm matter more than generic "about me" blocks.

Do not copy:
- Personal hero sections that hide actual work.
- Project grids with identical cards and no case hierarchy.

### Blog / Editorial

Observed examples include Felix blog, magazine layout, and blog web-section UI kits.

Reusable insight:
- Editorial templates need content taxonomy: featured story, categories, article cards, author metadata, newsletter capture, archive rhythm, and long-read readability.
- Typography, image crops, and category labels do more work than decorative UI effects.

Do not copy:
- Landing page CTA structure for blog products.
- Article grids with no editorial priority.

### Tech / SaaS / AI

Observed examples include Geri AI, Hyoga tech, Moren AI, Swift Host, Industri 4.0, and app development.

Reusable insight:
- Tech templates need concrete product proof: screenshots, workflow panels, integrations, security or performance trust, pricing, and onboarding logic.
- Dark or neon mood can work, but only when the UI evidence remains readable.

Do not copy:
- Abstract AI gradients with no product surface.
- Repeated dark bento cards that do not explain the product.

### Beauty / Wellness / Health

Observed examples include WellnessV2 and spa templates.

Reusable insight:
- These templates need warmth plus trust: service menu, practitioner or facility evidence, appointment flow, testimonials, location/contact, and safety/credibility cues.
- Human imagery or refined editorial illustration must feel calm and credible.

Do not copy:
- Decorative wellness vectors without service clarity.
- Low-contrast pastel text that looks gentle but becomes hard to read.

### Email Newsletter

Observed examples include agency and hair salon newsletters.

Reusable insight:
- Newsletter templates are not websites. They need email-width hierarchy, modular blocks, image-safe fallbacks, repeated campaign sections, and clear content priority.

Do not copy:
- Full website nav/hero behavior into an email layout.
- Wide desktop-only composition.

### Mobile App Screens

Observed examples include onboarding, login, insurance app, and mobile UI kit material.

Reusable insight:
- Mobile app templates need task flow, states, safe areas, controls, and screen-to-screen continuity.
- They should be routed as app UI or UI kit work, not website landing pages.

Do not copy:
- Selling a mobile screen set as if it were a scroll website.

## Website Design System Scope

For website template work, the design system should cover:

- Foundations: colors, typography, spacing, radius, shadow, icon style, grid, breakpoints.
- Navigation: desktop nav, mobile nav, active state, dropdown/menu behavior when relevant.
- Conversion controls: primary/secondary buttons, forms, inputs, search, filters, focus/error/disabled states.
- Content modules: hero, proof strip, feature blocks, process/timeline, testimonial, pricing, FAQ, footer.
- Media rules: hero images, product screenshots, editorial crops, gallery logic, avatar/logo handling.
- Product-specific modules: product cards, article cards, dashboard KPI cards, chart/table styles, appointment cards, destination cards, service cards.
- Responsive logic: first viewport priority, section reflow, mobile CTA placement, text wrapping, image crop behavior.
- Marketplace editability: named sections, replaceable imagery, realistic placeholder copy, reusable spacing rhythm.

## Human Craft Heuristics Learned

- Use rhythm variation: move between open hero space, dense proof, editorial crops, and modular details.
- Let one strong visual anchor lead: product photo, dashboard table, editorial image, destination crop, or real screenshot.
- Make the category obvious before asking for attention.
- Preserve restraint. The best templates do not decorate every section equally.
- Use believable sample content. Generic placeholder copy makes even polished visuals feel artificial.
- Create hierarchy surprise without breaking usability: offset image, side rail proof, split proof before features, product finder before benefits, case-study crop before services.

## Originality Rules For Future Website Templates

Before designing from this corpus:

- Diagnose subtype first: landing page, ecommerce, dashboard, portfolio, blog, newsletter, mobile app, hero kit, or SaaS.
- Choose a different first structural move from the closest reference.
- Change at least three of these: section order, composition, media strategy, component grouping, spacing rhythm, hierarchy anchor, visual motif.
- Keep mood-level learning only: energy, density, trust posture, imagery role, and marketplace completeness.
- Reject output if it only changes color, copy, and imagery while keeping the same section sequence.

## Acceptance Criteria

A strong website template should pass these checks:

- The first viewport communicates category and offer.
- The subtype has the right product evidence.
- The design system includes subtype-specific modules, not only generic buttons/cards.
- The section rhythm progresses instead of repeating one card/grid pattern.
- Desktop and mobile behavior are planned.
- Marketplace buyers can see what is editable and reusable.
- Similarity risk stays low against the closest reference.

## Routing Memory

Future prompts containing website, landing page, web template, ecommerce page, portfolio website, blog template, dashboard template, email newsletter, mobile app screen, or header kit must first route through medium/product-type diagnosis. Do not collapse all of them into the same landing page workflow.
