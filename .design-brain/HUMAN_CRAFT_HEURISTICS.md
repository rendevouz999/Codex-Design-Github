# Human Craft Heuristics

Use these heuristics to push designs beyond clean but generic output. These are not decoration rules; they are judgement prompts.

## Human Design Signals

- Clear intent: every section, slide, or screen has a reason to exist.
- Hierarchy surprise: one strong focal decision creates memorability without chaos.
- Rhythm variation: repeated modules change pacing through scale, alignment, density, or media.
- Layout fluidity: the page has a named spatial gesture, varied section silhouettes, controlled tension, and at least one meaningful rhythm break.
- Product specificity: visual choices fit buyer, niche, and medium rather than generic taste words.
- Constructive restraint: not every surface needs a border, card, shadow, or gradient.
- Imperfect symmetry with control: asymmetry creates energy while alignment keeps trust.
- Real content pressure: text, data, images, and placeholders feel like a real buyer could use them.
- Editorial pacing: important moments get more room; secondary details become supporting texture.
- Motif discipline: a shape, line, image crop, or accent appears as a system, not random ornament.
- Effect judgment: shadow, glow, blur, overlay, and border depth are used when they clarify hierarchy or polish, not because the reference had them or because the design feels empty.
- Finish quality: edge alignment, contrast, type scale, and spacing survive close inspection.

## Human Craft Questions

Ask before final:

- What is the one memorable move?
- What did I intentionally leave quiet?
- Where does the design breathe?
- Where does the design create tension?
- Does the structure fit the medium?
- Does the layout have a memorable gesture, or is it just a neat vertical stack?
- Would this page still look like a sibling of the previous template if I blurred the colors and text?
- Which section changes the silhouette, focal path, or module scale?
- Does every repeated element have a reason?
- Would a subtle effect improve hierarchy, depth, or premium feel, or would restraint be stronger?
- If the reference has effects, did I reinterpret their function instead of copying the values?
- Would a buyer understand how to edit this?
- Does it feel like a product, not a visual exercise?

## Common AI-Looking Failure Modes

- Everything is a rounded card.
- Every section uses the same bento grid.
- Mood words replace product logic.
- Mockups hide weak underlying screens.
- Gradients and glass effects appear without purpose.
- Effects are either absent because the agent forgot to consider them, or overused because the agent treated them as decoration.
- Text hierarchy uses too many sizes.
- All templates in a batch share the same structure.
- Templates differ in color and content but share the same zoomed-out silhouette.
- The page is polished but stiff: centered stack, repeated card rows, no focal-path shift, no controlled asymmetry, and no breakout moment.
- PowerPoint decks look like sliced landing pages.
- Design systems show colors but not usage rules.
